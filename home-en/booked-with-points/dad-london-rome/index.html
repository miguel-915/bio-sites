Booked With Points

I took my dad to London and Rome

My dad had never been to London. In January 2026 we fixed that: new American business class over, a night at the Park Hyatt, Rome the next day, and a long way home through Naples, Paris, Mexico City, and Zacatecas. Here is every number, exactly as it posted.

The totals, two travelers
  1. Cash paid: $2,781.47
  2. Points used: 192,000
  3. Plus 2 systemwide upgrades
  4. Retail price of the points legs: $12,000
Money saved: $10,908

Leg by leg

What each flight cost

Prices are what posted to my accounts and cards in January and February 2026. Retail prices are the cheapest business class fares we found for the same routes, and the cash fare for the London to Rome hop at the time we booked.

El Paso to Chicago to London, American Airlines

First class to Chicago, then the new American business class to London, one of the first routes flying it. I set alerts and grabbed the seats the day they opened.

Paid: $340.50 each, upgraded with systemwide upgrades earned from American status. Retail: from $3,500 each in business.

One night in London, Park Hyatt

My dad had never seen London, so we stopped for a night instead of connecting straight through.

Paid: $454.35 for the night, booked through Amex Fine Hotels and Resorts, with the $300 credit back. Net $154.35.

London to Rome, British Airways

An afternoon hop down to Rome to start the Italy part of the trip.

Paid: 13,000 Avios plus $2 each. The cash fare was about $150 each when we booked, close in. Today it prices from $99.

Rome to Naples, by train

We worked our way down Italy and took the train to Naples before flying home. Cash, small money, and the easiest leg of the trip.

Naples to Paris to Mexico City, Air France business

The long way home started with the flight I most wanted to get right. I had seen it for 60,000 miles the day before and paid more the next day. That stings, and it is also normal. Prices move.

Paid: 83,000 Flying Blue miles plus $353.41 each. Retail: from $2,350 each in business.

Mexico City to Zacatecas, Aeromexico business

Paid in cash: $233.18 each, converted from pesos at the January rate.

Zacatecas to Dallas to El Paso, American Airlines

Paid in cash: $384.47 each, Main Cabin Extra.

The math

Add the $300 back on the Park Hyatt through the FHR credit and the trip total comes to $10,908 saved from retail, using 192,000 points and 2 systemwide upgrades. Everything else on the trip, the train, the two cash flights home, and the hotel night itself, we paid for like anyone else.

How to read that number: money saved is measured against the retail price of the same flights. It only counts as a saving if you would have paid that price. We would have paid business class for the two long hauls to make this trip happen for my dad, so I count those. If you would have flown economy, your honest number is the economy price, and the system still works, just with smaller math.

How

Where the points came from

Here is where it all came from. The systemwide upgrades were earned from flying American, flight after flight, until the status added up. The miles and points came from regular credit card spend and from shopping through the American portal when I was buying things anyway. The Flying Blue miles were Amex points I transferred when the seats showed up. The Avios were already sitting in my account from earlier trips. Earn before you need them, then move fast when the seats open.

Could you book this today? The pieces move. The new American business class flies limited routes and upgrade space has to be open. Flying Blue prices change by the day, as my 60,000 versus 83,000 lesson shows. The Avios hop inside Europe is the easy part.
