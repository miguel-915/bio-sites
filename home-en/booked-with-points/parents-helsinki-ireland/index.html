Booked With Points

I took my parents to Helsinki and Ireland

In May 2025 I took my mom and dad to Finland and Ireland. Four of us flew up to Seattle, three of us crossed the ocean in business class, and we worked our way across Ireland from Kilkenny to Doolin to Dublin. Here is every number, exactly as it posted.

The totals, the whole trip
  1. Cash paid: $2,339.77
  2. Points used: 419,500
  3. Plus 1 free night certificate
  4. Retail price of the long haul flights: $15,900
Money saved on those flights: $15,657.57

Full leg by leg accounting below, including the hotels and the legs we paid cash for.

Leg by leg

What each part cost

Prices are what posted to my accounts and cards in May 2025. The retail prices in the math section are the cheapest business class fares on the same routes on other airlines. I picked those on purpose, to keep the comparison honest.

El Paso to Seattle, round trip, Alaska Airlines

Four of us flew this one: me, my parents, and my grandma. Two bookings, and on each one the second person flew on the Alaska companion fare, $99 plus taxes. My grandma stayed in Seattle while the three of us crossed the ocean, then flew home with us on the same round trip tickets.

Paid in cash: $603.63 per booking, $1,207.26 total, an average of $301.82 per person.

Seattle to Helsinki, Finnair business

The long haul out, booked with Alaska miles on Finnair. Out of Seattle in the evening, into Helsinki the next afternoon.

Paid: 55,000 Alaska miles plus $18.10 each. That is 165,000 miles and $54.30 total for the three of us. Retail: from $3,100 each in business.

One night in Helsinki, Hilton Helsinki Airport

We landed in the afternoon and flew out early the next morning, so we slept at the airport hotel.

Paid in cash: 179.55 euros, about $199.51, one room for the three of us.

Helsinki to Stockholm to Dublin, SAS economy

A short hop across to Ireland to start the drive, booked with Virgin points.

Paid: 15,500 Virgin points plus $28 each, 46,500 points and $84 total.

Mount Juliet Estate, County Kilkenny

The first night in Ireland, at a manor house estate outside Thomastown, booked with Marriott points. The folio shows 194 euros of food during the stay.

Paid: about 43,000 Marriott points for the room, plus 194 euros for food, about $215.57.

Doolin, The Ramblers Rest

A bed and breakfast in Doolin, the base for the Cliffs of Moher.

Paid in cash: $194 for the night.

Dublin, Staycity Aparthotels Augustine

The last night in Ireland, an aparthotel in Dublin before the flight home.

Paid in cash: $197 for the night.

Dublin to Minneapolis to Seattle, Aer Lingus business and Alaska First

The long haul home on the Aer Lingus A321neo to Minneapolis, a night there, then Alaska First to Seattle the next morning. One award booking covered both flights.

Paid: 55,000 Alaska miles plus $62.71 each. That is 165,000 miles and $188.13 total for the three of us. Retail: from $2,200 each for the pair of flights.

One night in Minneapolis, Hyatt

The Dublin flight landed at 5:30 in the evening and the Seattle flight left at 7 the next morning, so the night went on a Hyatt free night certificate. One room, two queen beds.

Paid: one free night certificate. No cash.

Seattle to El Paso, Alaska Airlines

The last flight home was the return half of the round trip tickets from the first leg, after a long layover in Seattle.

Paid: included in the round trip price above.

The math

What we booked with milesRetail
Seattle to Helsinki, business, three travelers$9,300
Dublin to Seattle, business and First, three travelers$6,600
Cash we actually paid on those flights$242.43
Money saved from retail$15,657.57

Those two flights are the whole math here on purpose. The SAS hop and the Mount Juliet night were also booked with points, and leaving them out keeps this number low instead of high. Across the whole trip we spent $2,339.77 in cash, 419,500 points and miles, and one free night certificate.

How to read that number: money saved is measured against the retail price, and it only counts as a saving if you would have paid that price. I compared against the cheapest business class seats on the same routes on other airlines, not the prices Finnair and Aer Lingus charge for their own seats, because I wanted the conservative number. If you would have flown economy, your honest number is the economy price, and the system still works, just with smaller math.

How

Where the points came from

Most of it started as Amex points. I moved Amex points over to top up the programs I needed, and the Alaska miles also came from my Alaska cards, from welcome bonuses and regular spend. The Virgin points and the Marriott points came from the same habit: earn in one place, move them when the seats show up, and book fast. The companion fares did the quiet work on the domestic flights. One full fare plus a $99 companion, twice, put four of us in Seattle and back for about $302 each.

Could you book this today? The shape of it, yes. The exact prices, no. Alaska has changed how it prices partner awards since this trip, and 55,000 miles for Finnair business is not the price to expect now. The companion fare still exists, and the hotels still take points and certificates.
